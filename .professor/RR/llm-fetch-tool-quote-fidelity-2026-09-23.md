# RR — How faithful are LLM fetch-and-answer tools at verbatim quoting, what failure modes are documented, and what mitigations work?

Question: How faithful are LLM "fetch a page and answer" tools (Claude Code WebFetch, OpenAI/Perplexity browsing, Jina Reader-style summarizers) when asked to quote a page verbatim — what failure modes (paraphrased or fabricated quotes, truncation, summarization-model limits) are documented or measured, and what mitigations work?

**Answer:** Quoting through these tools is not reliable, and no public benchmark tests any of the named fetch tools on verbatim quoting. What exists is adjacent evidence: news-accuracy studies (13% of quotes altered or missing in the BBC study), citation-support audits (51.5% of sentences supported), bug reports of silent truncation and of whole pages being invented, and designs that route the page through a second model before the answering model sees it. The mitigations with measured effect take the model out of the path of the quoted text: deterministic lookup of the quote, string-match checking of quotes against the raw source, citation APIs that return source spans, and extract-quotes-first or copy-oriented generation.

## Map

### 1. Tool pipelines: what the answering model actually sees
- **Claude Code WebFetch (reverse-engineered):** "HTML is converted to Markdown using the Turndown library." "The result is truncated to 100 KB of text with a warning if necessary." Then "A small, fast model runs with an empty system prompt (yes, it's totally empty)." That second-model instruction includes "Never produce or reproduce exact song lyrics." ([mikhail.io](https://mikhail.io/2025/10/claude-code-web-tools/)) So for most pages the main model receives a small model's answer, not the page text.
- **When WebFetch passes raw markdown instead:** "the domain must be preapproved, the content-type must be text/markdown, and the content must be under 100K characters. If any of those fail, you fall back to the Haiku pipeline like everyone else." The preapproved list is "a hardcoded whitelist of about 100 domains", and it has "No news sites… No Wikipedia, even." ([North Park Group](https://northparkgroup.co/blog/claude-code-leak-geo-analysis)) Anthropic's own descriptions contradict each other here: some configurations say WebFetch "returns the raw page as markdown inside `<fetched-web-content>` tags rather than a summary" ([claude-code #95127](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/95127)).
- **Anthropic API web_fetch server tool:** it retrieves full page text, truncated at an optional, approximate `max_content_tokens` limit. Citations are "optional for web fetch and disabled by default"; when enabled they return `char_location` spans with `cited_text` ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-fetch-tool)).
- **ChatGPT browsing (reverse-engineered, older `mclick` tool):** "The full page is not returned. Only a relevant chunk of the page." The chunk arrives as `tether_quote` excerpts ([sanand0 notes](https://github.com/sanand0/openai-conversations/blob/main/how-search-tool-works.md)).
- **Perplexity:** in Dow Jones v. Perplexity the plaintiffs showed examples in which "Perplexity 'fabricated' information not actually contained in the New York Post and Wall Street Journal articles that Perplexity cited" ([FindLaw opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dis-crt-sd-new-yor/117622090.html)). Android Authority reported that Perplexity "would sometimes make educated guesses based on article URLs instead of directly summarizing them" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/perplexity-uses-ai-content-sources-3455228/)). WIRED's honeypot finding ("Perplexity never actually looked at the page") is unquoted: only a secondary summary was read.
- **Jina Reader:** it "converts any URL to an LLM-friendly input". The markdown mode goes "without going through readability". On SPA or lazy-loaded pages "Reader may capture the preload content instead" ([jina-ai/reader README](https://github.com/jina-ai/reader/blob/main/README.md)). The LLM converter ReaderLM-v2 is opt-in (`x-engine: readerlm-v2`) and reports "ROUGE-L: 0.84", "Levenshtein Distance: 0.22" and "Jaro-Winkler Similarity: 0.82", so its markdown is not character-exact ([Hugging Face card](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/ReaderLM-v2)).

### 2. Measured fidelity of quotes and attribution
- **BBC (Feb 2025; ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity):** "13 percent of the quotes sourced from BBC articles were either altered from the original source or not present in the article cited", and "51 percent of all AI answers to questions about the news were judged to have significant issues of some form." ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/12/bbc_ai_news_accuracy/))
- **EBU/BBC (Oct 2025, 22 broadcasters):** "31% of all responses had significant problems with how they attributed information, including providing missing, incorrect, or entirely fabricated citations" ([Winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2025/10/22/ai-assistants-get-news-wrong-in-45-of-cases-landmark-bbc-ebu-study-finds-xcxwbn/)).
- **CJR Tow Center (Mar 2025, 8 AI search engines, excerpt-to-source task):** "Collectively, they provided incorrect answers to more than 60 percent of queries." Perplexity got "37 percent of the queries incorrectly" and Grok 3 "94 percent". "ChatGPT… incorrectly identified 134 articles, but signaled a lack of confidence just fifteen times out of its two hundred responses." ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php))
- **Quotes altered when the source is in context (newsroom tests):** all quotes were detected and left unaltered in "zero out of twelve for GPT-3.5 and six out of twelve for GPT-4". A two-step extract-then-rewrite prompt produced "no quotes were fabricated". ([Generative AI in the Newsroom](https://generative-ai-newsroom.com/towards-accurate-quote-aware-summarization-of-news-using-generative-ai-b786493e0c3d); UNCHECKED, 403 at verification.) Quote extraction with GPT-3.5 reached recall 0.77 and precision 0.74, and few-shot example quotes "reappeared multiple times in the final output" as invented attributions ([newsroom extraction study](https://generative-ai-newsroom.com/extracting-quotes-from-news-articles-with-llms-8e231aae77e7), unquoted in verification).
- **Verbatim does not mean supported:** a clinical QA study found `claude-opus-5` "quoted verbatim for 98.0% of its claims but fully substantiated only 37.1%" ([VeriQuote README](https://github.com/rickintoplace/veriquote)). SourceCheckup found "between 50% and 90% of LLM responses are not fully supported… Even for GPT-4o with Web Search, approximately 30% of individual statements are unsupported" ([Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58551-6)).
- **Documented WebFetch fabrication:** WebFetch "returned a full, detailed, internally-consistent job posting for a URL that a real browser confirms is a bare `500 Internal Server Error` page". This persisted under the prompt "Quote the raw text content of this page verbatim, character for character", and "an unattended scheduled job-search task emailed a 'verified live' job posting to a real user that was, in fact, a dead link" ([claude-code #87470](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/87470)).
- **The summarizer model as a proxy:** on Vectara's grounded-summarization leaderboard (prompt: "Summarize using only the information in the given passage…"), the row reads "anthropic/claude-haiku-4-5-20251001 | 9.8 % | 90.2 %" ([Vectara leaderboard](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)).

### 3. Citation and attribution benchmarks
- Generative search engines (Bing Chat, NeevaAI, perplexity.ai, YouChat): "a mere 51.5% of generated sentences are fully supported by citations" and "only 74.5% of citations support their associated sentence" ([arXiv 2304.09848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)).
- ALCE: "on the ELI5 dataset, even the best models lack complete citation support 50% of the time" ([arXiv 2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)).
- Quote-Tuning "significantly increases verbatim quotes from high-quality documents by up to 130% relative to base models", which means base models do not quote verbatim by default ([arXiv 2404.03862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03862)).
- URLs: "3–13% of citation URLs are hallucinated" and "5–18% are non-resolving overall" across 10 commercial models and deep-research agents ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1)).

### 4. Truncation and extraction failures
- Silent truncation: "the tool's own boundary note reported returning 39,415 of 502,907 characters — 7.8% of the document". "The words truncate, limit, size and length do not appear" in the tool description. "The calling model sees an answer about 7.8% of a document presented exactly like an answer about all of it." ([claude-code #95127](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/95127)) A Firebase docs page was cut off mid-sentence with no marker, and the missing configuration was reported as absent rather than unread ([claude-code #22937](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/22937), unquoted in verification).
- Position effects: performance "significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts" ([arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)).
- Extraction artifacts: mid-sentence line breaks, ligatures, hyphenation, page numbers and chunk boundaries make faithful quotes "fail substring validation" ([quant-mind #157](https://github.com/LLMQuant/quant-mind/issues/157)). Extractors disagree on what the page text is: trafilatura 2.2.0 scores F 0.924 and readability-lxml F 0.826 ([Trafilatura evaluation](https://trafilatura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/evaluation.html)).
- JavaScript: "none of the main LLM bots… were able to render JavaScript", so client-built content is invisible to them ([Visively](https://visively.com/kb/ai/ai-crawlers-javascript-rendering)). Some tools say so, while Perplexity has been seen guessing from the URL (see §1). The two behaviours are both documented for different tools and cases.

### 5. Mitigations and evidence they work
- **Deterministic quoting:** the LLM emits only reference IDs and a non-AI lookup inserts the verbatim text. Quote hallucinations were 0, and hallucinations outside quotes fell from 12% to 2%, on N=60 (non-public data) ([Matt Yeung](https://mattyyeung.github.io/deterministic-quoting)).
- **String-match checking of quotes against the source:** VeriQuote reports that "Faithfully copied quotes never score below 0.660, quotes that are not in the source never above 0.396", and that "The matcher blocks all 187 quotes that are not in the source and none of the 586 real quotes". However, "even the best judge lets one unsupported citation in six through as fully supported". Matching catches invented text, not misuse of real text ([VeriQuote](https://github.com/rickintoplace/veriquote)). The checker must see the raw source: a summarizer-mediated fetch (§1) cannot be matched against.
- **Citations API:** it is "increasing recall accuracy by up to 15%". Endex reports "we reduced source hallucinations and formatting issues from 10% to 0%". Anthropic's wording is "Cited text will reference source documents to minimize hallucinations", which stops short of an explicit verbatim guarantee ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/introducing-citations-api)). The same feature exists on web_fetch but is off by default (§1).
- **Extract quotes first, then answer:** Anthropic's long-context tests showed "a 36% reduction in errors" from pulling quotes into a scratchpad ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/prompting-long-context)). The newsroom two-step prompt produced no fabricated quotes (§2, UNCHECKED).
- **Copy-oriented generation:** CopyPasteLLM beats baselines by "12.6, 12.2, and 24.5 percentage points" on FaithEval, and raises ConFiQA average accuracy "from 84.49% to 94.37%" ([arXiv 2510.00508](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00508)).
- **Fetch raw and parse it yourself instead of relying on a summarizer:** no measured comparison was found. The case for it rests on the pipeline facts in §1 and §4.
