# RR — Revenue models of free, sponsor-funded multi-city tech meetup networks (AI Tinkerers, MLOps Community, PyData/NumFOCUS, Pavilion, Women Who Code) and what transfers to AI Builders Club

Question: Wave 2 of a study on the next best move for AI Builders Club. Lens: REVENUE MODEL. Cases: AI Tinkerers, MLOps Community, PyData/NumFOCUS, Pavilion, Women Who Code (failure), plus 1-2 closer analogues. Questions: (1) what sponsors pay and the HQ/chapter split; (2) each case's full revenue mix with numbers and dates; (3) what a PyData event nets, NumFOCUS fiscal-sponsor fee vs Open Collective Europe; (4) Pavilion going paid and cutting chapter-lead revenue share; (5) Women Who Code: which funding dried up, which fixed costs killed it, warning signs for us; (6) which revenue lines scale with city count and which break the free/builder culture. Return: saved path, per-case cited mechanics, a "transferable to AI Builders" table (move, evidence, expected EUR range, risk, fit with the two-entity model), open rabbit holes.

## Answer

None of the comparable free networks publishes a per-chapter revenue split. The working pattern is the same everywhere:
- **AI Tinkerers:** HQ sells global and digital deals, and single-city money is "route[d] to that local organizer team" ([AI Tinkerers](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)).
- **MLOps Community:** HQ helps organizers "find sponsors to pay for the food and drinks" after a central budget of "six or seven grand a month" for 37 cities proved too costly ([MLOps Community](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **PyData:** surplus comes from flagship conferences priced £4,000–£17,000 per sponsor, while local meetups are kept free ([PyData London](https://pydata.org/london2026/sponsor), [PyData meetups](https://pydata.org/start-a-meetup/)).

The two cautionary cases fail on fixed obligations, not on sponsorship as such:
- **Pavilion** paid "millions of dollars" to chapter heads, then called moving away from chapters "a big mistake" ([Baxter interview](https://robbiekellmanbaxter.com/blog/scaling-membership-for-the-long-game-with-pavilion-founder-sam-jacobs/)).
- **Women Who Code** grew salaries "by nearly $900,000 from 2021 to 2022" into a deficit of "more than $190,000", then dissolved ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/business/atlanta-based-women-who-code-abruptly-shuts-down-due-to-finances/KH5LEQXBAFATRFM6EM3SF726JQ/)).

## Map

### 1. AI Tinkerers (for-profit-adjacent network, venture fund alongside)

**How sponsorship is sold**
- **Three paths.** Global Partnership, Sponsor Local Chapter and Digital Programs.
- **Local money goes to the local team.** "Support one city's next meetup, dinner, or technical gathering. Tell us the city and we'll route it to that local organizer team." ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor))
- **Local organizers keep a veto.** "Local organizers decide whether it fits their calendar and community." ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor))
- **HQ controls access to cities.** "Many cities may not be available outside of a global partnership." ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor))
- **Scale.** "264+ cities worldwide" ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)).
- **Digital programs.** "Newsletter sponsorships and targeted placements. Guest posts and technical partner content. Podcast or video interview sponsorship. Webinars with a builder-relevant technical angle. Surveys and audience insight programs." ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor))

**Prices**
- **None published.** The current page gives no dollar price for any sponsorship ([aitinkerers.org/sponsor](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)). The Jan 2026 snapshot lists each line as "Price: Contact us" ([Wayback 2026-01-28](https://web.archive.org/web/20260128020331/https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)).
- **Inquiry budget selector.** Its ranges run up to "$250k+" (unquoted).
- **Organizer duty.** The same snapshot says: "The AI Tinkerers Organizer will handle food and beverage for attendees."
- **Repeat sponsors.** It names "OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Microsoft, Amazon, Neon, Groq, Samsung, Okta, Weights and Biases, Cloudflare, ElevenLabs, Comet" ([Wayback 2026-01-28](https://web.archive.org/web/20260128020331/https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)).

**Monetization**
- **2024: none.** "AI Tinkerers is not monetized. The only money — and the only way VCs do get in — comes from sponsors who pick up the tab for a meetup venue and the food and drink." ([GeekWire, 2024-04-19](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/), UNCHECKED, 403 at verification)

- **Fund leadership.** Founder Joe Heitzeberg is listed as founding partner: "he built the network this fund runs on" ([aitfund.ai](https://aitfund.ai/)).

**Organizer pay and split.** No source states organizer pay or an HQ/chapter split (dug, unsettled).

### 2. MLOps Community (chapters moved from HQ-funded to locally sponsored)

- **Original chapter funding.** Organizers were told "you can expense the community up to $1,200" ([MLOps 4-years video](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **The cost problem.** "we're paying, like, six or seven grand a month just for these meetups and the Neptune contract" ([same](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **Scale at the time.** "local meetups that are happening in 37 cities" ([same](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **New model.** "we help you find sponsors to pay for the food and drinks" ([same](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **A global sponsor.** "We sold it to Neptune" ([same](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)). The contract amount is undisclosed.
- **Staff.** "there's about seven direct employees of the community" ([same](https://home.mlops.community/public/videos/looking-back-on-4-years-of-the-mlops-community)).
- **Later scale.** "31k Slack members" and "600+ events in 45 cities" ([MLOps Community 2.0](https://mlops.community/blog/mlops-community-2-0), unquoted).
- **Governance.** The same page describes a governing board and committees with Linux Foundation governance (unquoted).
- **Unknown.** Sponsor, conference and job-board prices, legal entity, and revenue totals.

### 3. PyData / NumFOCUS, with Open Collective Europe (now "Open Source Europe") for comparison

**Conference sponsorship**
- **PyData London 2026 tiers.** "£17,000 Diamond", "£12,000 Platinum", "£8,000 Gold", "£4,000 Silver" ([PyData London 2026](https://pydata.org/london2026/sponsor)).
- **Where the surplus goes.** "NumFOCUS reinvests profits from our events to directly support NumFOCUS's sponsored projects" ([same](https://pydata.org/london2026/sponsor)).
- **Organizer guidance.** "Any and all proceeds made from PyData events will benefit NumFOCUS…" ([PyData organize](https://pydata.org/organize-a-conference/)).
- **Cost coverage.** "Sponsorship funding should cover most or all of the costs for the venue, catering, AV/videography, and keynote speaker travel expenses." ([PyData organize](https://pydata.org/organize-a-conference/))
- **No net figure.** No PyData net or surplus figure was found.

**NumFOCUS totals (Form 990)**
- **2022:** revenue $9,289,091, expenses $5,744,317.
- **2023:** revenue $7,245,742, expenses $9,951,106.
- **2024:** revenue $8,376,226, expenses $11,090,430.
- Source for all three: [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/454547709), unquoted. PyData is not itemized.

**Meetups**
- **NumFOCUS pays the platform.** "NumFOCUS will pay the monthly cost of the Meetup website" ([PyData meetups](https://pydata.org/start-a-meetup/)).
- **Events stay free.** "members may not be charged a membership fee or any entry fee for events without prior written approval" ([same](https://pydata.org/start-a-meetup/)).
- **Organizers are not paid.** No payment to organizers was found.

**Fees**
- **NumFOCUS on grants.** "NumFOCUS charges a 15% indirect/overhead fee for all grants" ([NumFOCUS Finance KB](https://finance.numfocus.org/kb/article/114-apply-for-and-manage-grants-through-numfocus/), UNCHECKED, SSL failure at verification).
- **NumFOCUS on donations.** A 10% fee on donations is snippet-only (open).
- **Open Source Europe (the opencollective.com/europe page).**
  - "8% + Stripe/PayPal fees for online contributions"
  - "10% for bank transfers"
  - "10% for tax-deductible donations and grants"
  - "Open Source Europe is a nonprofit based in Belgium."
  - Source for all four: [opencollective.com/europe](https://opencollective.com/europe).
- **Open Collective Foundation.** Its board decided "to dissolve the Open Collective Foundation (OCF), effective December 31, 2024" ([OCF announcement, 2024-02-28](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/announcement-we-are-dissolving-open-collective-foundation-at-the-end-of-this-year)).
- **Comparison.** Open Source Europe's 8% plus processor fees on card money is below NumFOCUS's 15% on grants. Both sit around 10% on grants and donations.

### 4. Pavilion (formerly Revenue Collective; paid membership)

**Going paid**
- **Dues start.** "I started charging dues on January 1st, 2018." ([GTMnow, 2023-05-03](https://gtmnow.com/podcast-using-kindness-to-build-business-sam-jacobs/))
- **First sponsor.** "I sold my first sponsorship for Revenue Collective to the company Salesloft" ([GTMnow, 2023-05-03](https://gtmnow.com/podcast-using-kindness-to-build-business-sam-jacobs/)).
- **Growth to 2021.** Jacobs "bootstrapped the company to $10 million in ARR before taking on $25 million in growth financing, led by Elephant Ventures and GTM Fund in early 2021" ([GTMnow, 2023-05-03](https://gtmnow.com/podcast-using-kindness-to-build-business-sam-jacobs/)).
- **Current pricing.** Pricing is "based on your seniority and location" ([Pavilion pricing](https://www.joinpavilion.com/pricing)). The original dues price was not found.

**Chapter heads: paid, then cut, then reversed**
- **The revenue share.** "we were pretty generous with revenue shares" and "we were paying millions of dollars to those chapter heads" ([Baxter interview, recorded July 2023](https://robbiekellmanbaxter.com/blog/scaling-membership-for-the-long-game-with-pavilion-founder-sam-jacobs/)).
- **The effect of moving away from chapters.** "our growth was slowing down. And our member satisfaction was nominally declining" ([same](https://robbiekellmanbaxter.com/blog/scaling-membership-for-the-long-game-with-pavilion-founder-sam-jacobs/)).
- **The reversal.** "we kind of botched this transition, and we got away from our roots. And we're realizing now that it was a big mistake, and we're bringing chapters back." ([same](https://robbiekellmanbaxter.com/blog/scaling-membership-for-the-long-game-with-pavilion-founder-sam-jacobs/))
- **On price.** "I will admit, I think we're a little expensive right now" ([same](https://robbiekellmanbaxter.com/blog/scaling-membership-for-the-long-game-with-pavilion-founder-sam-jacobs/)).
- **The relaunch.** "In the second half of 2023, we re-introduced local community to Pavilion, beginning with our flagship chapters in London and New York." ([Pavilion 2023 review](https://www.joinpavilion.com/blog/2023-year-in-review))

**Staff cuts.** "Pavilion went from 62 employees in 2023 to 27 in 2024", with "the elimination of the product and engineering organization" ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/pavilion/contacts); an aggregator, single source).

**Community events.** "PavilionX events may charge fees for attendance to cover costs, and accept sponsorships of $1,500 or under, but all fee and sponsorship funds must go directly towards covering the cost of the event" ([PavilionX](https://www.joinpavilion.com/pavilionx)).

**Unknown.** The share percentage and the date of the cut.

**Closure**

- **The stated reason.** "factors that have materially impacted our funding sources — funds that were critical to continuing our programming" ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/business/atlanta-based-women-who-code-abruptly-shuts-down-due-to-finances/KH5LEQXBAFATRFM6EM3SF726JQ/); [Electronics Weekly](https://www.electronicsweekly.com/news/business/women-who-code-shuts-down-2024-04/)).
- **Which sponsors left.** No source names the sponsors that withdrew.

**Girls in Tech (a separate network, closed for the same reason)**
- **What happened.** "To have five recurring corporate sponsors pull out all at the same time–that's a jolt to the system." ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/why-girls-in-tech-had-to-close-adriana-gascoigne-interview/))
- **Its funding source.** "Primarily corporate sponsorships, where we got the majority of our funding–companies are just focused on cost containment." ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/why-girls-in-tech-had-to-close-adriana-gascoigne-interview/))
- **Sector context.** Girls in Tech shut down in July 2024, and Tech Talent Charter closed in June 2024 ([Forbes Council, 2024-10-24](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesnonprofitcouncil/2024/10/24/organizations-that-support-women-in-tech-are-struggling-heres-how-that-can-be-reversed/)).

**Warning signs to watch at AI Builders, inferred from the two cases above**
1. Payroll growing faster than revenue. Women Who Code's expenses rose about $1.14M in one year while revenue fell.
2. A handful of recurring corporate renewals carrying the budget. Girls in Tech lost five at once.
3. Volunteer chapters with no visibility into HQ finances.

### 7. Synthesis: which lines scale with city count, and which break the culture (inference from the map; not a sourced claim)

**Scale with city count**
- **Local night sponsorship.** Grows roughly linearly, but only if each city has its own seller. MLOps Community shows the cost side is also linear when HQ pays.
- **Multi-city and global packages.** Grow faster than city count. AI Tinkerers withholds cities "outside of a global partnership", so footprint itself is what is sold.
- **Digital programs.** Scale with audience, not with cities.

**Do not scale with city count**
- **Flagship conferences** (PyData pattern).
- **Job boards**, which scale with members (Superpath).

**Lines that break the free/builder culture**
- **Paid entry.** PyData bans entry fees without approval.
- **Paid membership.** Pavilion now says it is "a little expensive", and Rosie Sherry says "Membership is tough".
- **Recurring cash to chapter leads.** Pavilion's "millions" became a liability once it had to be cut.
- **Sponsor-driven promotion.** Rosie Sherry warns against "companies come in trying to advertise" (unquoted).
