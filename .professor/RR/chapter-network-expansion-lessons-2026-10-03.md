# RR — How chapter networks (City.AI, Founder Institute, HackerNest, Codebar, AI Tinkerers, GDG, Startup Grind) launch, govern, incentivize and lose chapters, and whether AI Builders Club should expand cities or deepen hubs

Question: Wave 2, GROWTH & EXPANSION lens for AI Builders Club (Amsterdam nonprofit, ~9 running cities, volunteer organizers promised "virtual shares"). Cases: City.AI, Founder Institute, HackerNest, Codebar, AI Tinkerers, plus GDG and Startup Grind. Q1: how each network approves, launches, governs and shuts down chapters. Q2: what keeps organizers going, and churn. Q3: City.AI and HackerNest after their peaks. Q4: Founder Institute chapter equity and whether it can underpin virtual shares. Q5: how AI Tinkerers reached 264 cities and how many are active. Q6: evidence on growth versus depth.

## Answer

No network studied pays its organizers in cash. Founder Institute is the one with real economics: its local leaders get [1% of every graduate's 2.5% warrant plus a percentage of fees](https://fi.co/lead-a-chapter). The warrant only pays out at [an outside equity round of $100,000 or more](https://fi.co/faq). The others run on status and a commitment:
- GDG organizers ["are not compensated by Google"](https://gdg.community.dev/participation-terms/).
- Startup Grind directors serve ["a minimum 2 year endeavor on a pro-bono basis"](https://www.startupgrind.com/blog/startup-grind-stockholm-is-looking-for-its-next-chapter-director/).
- HackerNest organizers committed ["for a year or two (about 4 or 6 events a calendar year)"](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321123431/http://hackernest.com/faqs/).

Wide networks carry many inactive chapters:
- AI Tinkerers reports ["114 — Active Cities (Last 6 Months)"](https://nyc.aitinkerers.org/organize-a-city) against [264 listed cities](https://aitinkerers.org/all_cities).
- Girl Develop It had [60 chapters, "only five" of them active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Develop_It), in July 2019.
- Association chapter leaders [turn over every 1-2 years](https://www.i4a.com/blog/association-chapter-management/).

So the evidence favours deepening a few dense hubs and adding cities only behind an activity gate. A Dutch stichting cannot simply pay organizers "virtual shares": its purpose may not include [payments to founders or board members](https://www.viajuridica.nl/informatiesoorten/wetstoelichtingen/definitie-stichting-art-2-285-bw).

### 2. Founder Institute (Q1, Q2, Q4)

**How launch works**
- The steps are "Apply in five minutes", review and interview, a six-session free virtual Accelerator Lab, then launch.
- The rule is "One leader team per city", at "6–10 hours a week", across "200+ cities" ([lead-a-chapter](https://fi.co/lead-a-chapter)).
- No termination, performance or leaver terms are published.

**How the equity works**
- Founders pledge "2.5% of their future equity in the form of a Warrant".
- It splits 0.5% to mentors, 1% to FI HQ and 1% to local leaders.
- "majority of financial returns to the Equity Collective (60%) go back to your Local Leaders and Mentors".
- "Over $8.5M in Equity Collective contributions have been distributed by FI HQ" (undated) ([fi.co/equity](https://fi.co/equity)).

**Trigger and the 4% to 2.5% change**
- "The Warrant grants the Founder Institute 2.5% at the time of the first Qualified Equity Financing".
- A qualified financing is "any equity investment for $100,000 USD or more completed by external investors".
- "To make the Equity Collective more accessible, we reduced it to 2.5%".
- Founders who enrolled "before February 2, 2022" signed the old 4% terms ([FI FAQ](https://fi.co/faq)) — all confirmed.

**Earlier terms.** The older director deck says "Graduates contribute 4% in warrants to a 15-year pool", split 25% each to directors, mentors, graduates and FI ([SlideShare](https://slideshare.net/FounderInstitute/become-a-founder-institute-director)) — unquoted.

**Fee share.** "A percentage goes to you: real economics from your first cohort"; the percentage itself is not published ([lead-a-chapter](https://fi.co/lead-a-chapter)).

### 3. Virtual shares under Dutch nonprofit law (Q4)

- **The stichting ban.** Art. 2:285 lid 3 BW reads: "Het doel van de stichting mag niet inhouden het doen van uitkeringen aan oprichters of aan hen die deel uitmaken van haar organen noch ook aan anderen, tenzij wat deze laatsten betreft de uitkeringen een ideële of sociale strekking hebben." ([viajuridica](https://www.viajuridica.nl/informatiesoorten/wetstoelichtingen/definitie-stichting-art-2-285-bw)). In English: a stichting's purpose may not include payments to its founders or board members, nor to others unless the payment serves an idealistic or social aim.
- **Tax definition of phantom shares.** "De virtuele aandelen zijn geen aandelen in het kapitaal van de werkgever. Ze geven geen stemrecht en/of recht op dividend en zijn niet vrij verhandelbaar." This is framed for employees only ([Belastingdienst KG](https://kennisgroepen.belastingdienst.nl/publicaties/kg204202514-genietingsmoment-virtuele-aandelen/)). In English: virtual shares are not shares in the employer's capital, carry no vote or dividend right, and cannot be freely traded.
- **Volunteer allowance cap.** For 2026 it is "€ 220 per maand / € 2.200 per jaar", or "€ 5,75 per uur" ([stichting.nl](https://www.stichting.nl/maximaal-vrijgestelde-vrijwilligersvergoeding/)).
- **One ANBI's board pay policy.** "Bestuurders ontvangen conform de gestelde wetgeving geen beloning, maar kunnen eventueel gemaakte onkosten vergoed krijgen." ([example ANBI policy](https://www.worldwideoutreach.net/anbi/beloningsbeleid)). In English: in line with the law, board members receive no pay, though expenses may be reimbursed.
- **The commercial-BV route.** Housing virtual shares in a commercial BV beside the stichting is unverified; no source confirms it.

### 4. City.AI (Q1, Q3)

**Model**
- Co-founder Mike Reiner, on the 2019-06 podcast: "Initially we just reached out to friends of ours and asked 'Hey do you want to start a chapter in your city?' So we worked with what we call ambassadors in the respective cities, they started a local chapter."
- "we are now close to 200 ambassadors" in a Slack group.
- "Now, we are in like 57 cities."
- "it really started just as a passion project."
- "on the back of it, we started a conference called WorldSummit.AI" ([appselekt podcast](https://www.appselekt.com/podcasts/e30-mike-reiner-cofounder-city-ai-2-of-2)) — confirmed.

**Scale timeline**

- "280+ local ambassadors based on 70+ cities" in January 2021 ([Wayback 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://city.ai/)).

**After the peak**
- The 2023-08-29 snapshot still shows the same 2021 text ("280+ local ambassadors … 70+ cities") and promotes "Global AI Festival 2021" ([Wayback 2023](http://web.archive.org/web/20230829061657/https://city.ai/)).
- The site is frozen rather than shut. No wind-down statement or founder post-mortem was found.
- **DISPUTED legal form.** Its founders call City.AI an NPO ([Wayback 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/2018/https://city.ai/about/)), while Crunchbase lists it as for-profit, as relayed by [Moltzau](https://alexmoltzau.medium.com/city-ai-the-global-initiative-near-you-29639f398c9).

### 5. HackerNest (Q1, Q2, Q3)

**Approval and team**
- Approval is a call: "We'll do a call to manage expectations and make sure everyone understands the flavor/intent/vibe/message/spirit we're pushing and go from there."
- Organizers "tend to be teams of 3 or more technologists".
- "City Organizers typically agree to help run a city for a year or two (about 4 or 6 events a calendar year)", with Tech Socials "every 2-3 months to start".
- Volunteers "are definitely our first choices when we seek potential Organizers".
- "We aspire to have one 'splinter cell' per city."
- "We're putting together a HackerNest instruction manual", so the playbook was still being written in 2016.

**What organizers got**
- "Do I have to pay to be an organizer? Nope."
- The perks were status and credibility: "you'll end up becoming very, very central in the community".

**Money**
- "Currently, we stay afloat on sponsorship and 'pay what you can' door donations."
- "As of May 2014, we're in over 15 cities".
- All of the above is from the [HackerNest FAQ, archived 2016-03-21](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321123431/http://hackernest.com/faqs/) — confirmed.

**Peak.** "As of July 2017, HackerNest splinter cells have run over 550+ events in 34 cities across 14 countries on 5 continents"; it was "founded on January 11, 2011" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HackerNest)) — confirmed.

**After the peak**
- The current homepage describes "A grassroots nonprofit that brought together tech communities in cities across the globe" (past tense).
- It offers "a limited set of programs and events" plus "Bespoke hackathon production advisory & coaching".
- It lists undated totals of "1,300+ Live Events", "68+ Cities" and "900+ Volunteers" ([hackernest.com](https://hackernest.com)) — unquoted.
- No source explains why the chapters stopped.

### 7. Growth versus depth (Q6)

**Girl Develop It (a breadth collapse)**
- "In 2017, the organization reported chapters in 53 cities".
- "As of July 2019 GDI lists 55,000 members in 60 chapters ... However, only five of these chapters are actively organizing classes and events."
- The trigger was governance: "Chapter Leaders ... released an open letter to GDI HQ and board, demanding change in leadership" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Develop_It)) — confirmed.

**Association chapters (2025 Mariner benchmark)**
- "77% of headquarters staff and 63% of chapter leaders cite 'insufficient qualified volunteers' as the biggest challenge".
- Volunteer leaders "turn over every 1-2 years".
- "Fewer than 20% attend chapter events" ([I4A](https://www.i4a.com/blog/association-chapter-management/)) — confirmed.

**AI Tinkerers.** About 43% of listed cities were active in the last six months ([organize-a-city](https://nyc.aitinkerers.org/organize-a-city)) — UNCHECKED.

**Women Who Code.** It shut down in April 2024 "due to a lack of sufficient funding" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesnonprofitcouncil/2024/10/24/organizations-that-support-women-in-tech-are-struggling-heres-how-that-can-be-reversed/)) — unquoted. This is sponsor dependence, with no stated link to chapter breadth.

**Density.** "Bangalore's startup community is more productive ... because the network is denser" ([startupsusa.org](https://startupsusa.org/new-evidence-on-fostering-productive-startup-communities/)) — unquoted. This is about an ecosystem, not a meetup network.

**Gap.** No source compared member retention or sponsor value between dense hubs and wide networks directly.

### Transferable to AI Builders

| Move | Evidence | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Activity gate: a city counts as "running" only with an event in the last 6 months; publish the active number, as AI Tinkerers does (114 of 264) | [AIT organize-a-city](https://nyc.aitinkerers.org/organize-a-city) (UNCHECKED); GDI 5 of 60 active ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Develop_It)) | A smaller headline number; the "82 cities" slide claim would need to go |
| Launch only with a team of 2-3 people, a booked first venue and sponsor, and a stated term (1-2 years, 4-10 events a year) | HackerNest teams of 3+ for one to two years ([FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321123431/http://hackernest.com/faqs/)); Codebar host for the first two workshops ([manual](https://manual.codebar.io/bringing-codebar-to-your-area.html)); Startup Grind 2 years and 8-10 events ([post](https://www.startupgrind.com/blog/startup-grind-stockholm-is-looking-for-its-next-chapter-director/)) | Slower city count; solo founders in new cities get turned away |
| Plan for organizer handover every 1-2 years with a co-lead pipeline drawn from volunteers | Turnover every 1-2 years ([I4A](https://www.i4a.com/blog/association-chapter-management/)); HackerNest picks organizers from its volunteers ([FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20160321123431/http://hackernest.com/faqs/)) | Needs written playbooks; HackerNest's was still unfinished in 2016 |
| Replace "virtual shares" with something legal and concrete: an FI-style pool of startup warrants or carry, or a plan in a separate BV; inside the stichting, cap cash at the €2,200/yr volunteer allowance | FI 1% leader share, triggered at a $100k round ([fi.co/equity](https://fi.co/equity), [FAQ](https://fi.co/faq)); stichting ban ([viajuridica](https://www.viajuridica.nl/informatiesoorten/wetstoelichtingen/definitie-stichting-art-2-285-bw)); allowance ([stichting.nl](https://www.stichting.nl/maximaal-vrijgestelde-vrijwilligersvergoeding/)) | The BV route is unverified and needs a Dutch lawyer; FI's payouts depend on startup exits years later |
| Neutrality rule plus a builders-only screen to protect room quality as sponsors grow | AIT neutrality rule and "100% active builder" policy ([quickstart](https://aitinkerers.org/p/ai-tinkerers-city-organizer-quickstart-guide), [FAQ](https://aitinkerers.org/p/ai-tinkerers-faq)) | Turns away paying vendors |
| Sell sponsors depth in named hubs, plus a multi-city package | AIT global tier and local deals ([sponsors](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsors)); Women Who Code funding collapse ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesnonprofitcouncil/2024/10/24/organizations-that-support-women-in-tech-are-struggling-heres-how-that-can-be-reversed/)) | Concentrates risk on a few sponsors |
| Target cities where AI Tinkerers is stale (Berlin, Hamburg in the sample) rather than where it is fresh (London, Amsterdam) | Digger's 13-city sample (unquoted, fetch-model readings) | A one-day snapshot; activity changes month to month |

**Verdict: deepen hubs, and add cities only through an activity gate.**
- The wide networks studied show large inactive tails: AI Tinkerers at about 43% active (UNCHECKED), Girl Develop It at 5 of 60, HackerNest moved to consulting, and City.AI frozen at its 2021 page.
- Their organizers work unpaid, and association data puts volunteer leader turnover at 1-2 years.
- Each new city therefore adds a recurring burden of handing the city to a new leader; it is not a one-off launch.
- AI Tinkerers already holds 264 listed cities, so AI Builders cannot win on breadth. Its own sampled pages show gaps in cities such as Berlin and Hamburg.
- This verdict rests on failure and dormancy cases plus one benchmark. No source compared retention or sponsor value directly between dense and wide networks, so treat it as a strong inference, not a measured result.
