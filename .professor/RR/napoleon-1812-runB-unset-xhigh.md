# RR — What destroyed the Grande Armée in 1812, and what would a time-traveller bring?

Question: What actually destroyed Napoleon's Grande Armée in the 1812 Russian campaign — cold, typhus carried by lice, trench fever, starvation, desertion, or the legendary tin buttons crumbling in the frost? Weigh the modern evidence (including the 2001 Vilnius mass-grave DNA studies) against the popular myths, and say what a time-traveller would need to bring back to 1812 to save the most soldiers.

Disease did most of the killing, and much of it happened before the frost. One estimate puts the loss at 80,000 soldiers to typhus and dysentery [one month into the campaign](https://www.montana.edu/historybug/napoleon/typhus-russia.html), and up to [220,000 dead from disease alone](https://www.montana.edu/historybug/napoleon/typhus-russia.html). DNA from the Vilnius grave shows louse-borne trench fever and typhus ([Raoult 2006](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-00008119)) and also paratyphoid and louse-borne relapsing fever ([Barbieri 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/10/251026021727.htm)). The tin-button story does not hold up: tin pest needs [about 18 months](https://www.straightdope.com/21343875/where-did-we-get-the-idea-crazy-people-think-they-re-napoleon-plus-did-tin-disease-contribute-to-nap) to do real damage, and the grave's tin-alloy buttons are [still legible](https://www.straightdope.com/21343875/where-did-we-get-the-idea-crazy-people-think-they-re-napoleon-plus-did-tin-disease-contribute-to-nap). A time-traveller should bring a way to kill lice and a way to make water safe, not winter coats or tin-free buttons. This last point is an inference from the disease evidence; no source tests it directly.

## Map

### Vilnius DNA evidence
- Raoult et al. 2006 (J Infect Dis 193:112-120): lice segments were found in the soil, and "B. quintana (the agent of trench fever) was identified by PCR and sequencing in 3 of the lice". Dental pulp "from the remains of 35 soldiers revealed DNA of B. quintana in 7 soldiers and DNA of R. prowazekii in 3 other soldiers". The authors call this "nearly one-third" of the soldiers. [HAL abstract](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-00008119), quoted by a digger, not re-checked in verification.
- Barbieri et al. 2025 (Current Biology) tested 13 soldiers exhumed in 2002: "DNA from S. enterica Paratyphi C was found in four individuals"; "B. recurrentis was detected in two." [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/10/251026021727.htm). A digger reported that this study found no typhus or trench fever DNA. That claim was NOT ON PAGE at verification, so it is not used here.

### Loss timeline and causes
- Strength: "initial force upon entering Russia exceeded 450,000 men" [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia). By the Berezina there were "around 49,000 troops alongside 40,000 stragglers", and the losses were "approximately 380,000 lives (dead or missing/prisoner)" [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia). Minard's map gives 422,000 men crossing the border and about 100,000 reaching Moscow [Significance](https://significancemagazine.com/napoleon-s-russian-campaign-200-years-on-2/), unquoted.
- Disease before the cold: "Just one month into the campaign, Napoleon had lost 80,000 soldiers to typhus and dysentery." "As many as 220,000 may have died solely from disease." "400,000 soldiers may have died from illness, exposure, or battlefield injuries." [Montana State](https://www.montana.edu/historybug/napoleon/typhus-russia.html)
- Horses: about 175,000 lost [Warfare History Network](https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=36523), unquoted. Prisoners: 150,000 to 200,000 [Pugaciauskas 2012](https://www.cahiers-lituaniens.org/PUGACIAUSKAS-Le-passage-de-Napoleon-a-Vilnius-en-1812-n-11-2012.pdf), unquoted. The Berezina crossing cost more than 20,000 men [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/october-19/napoleon-retreats-moscow), unquoted. Temperatures fell to about −32 to −37°C in early December [NZ Geographic](https://www.nzgeo.com/?p=7026), unquoted.
- Desertion and starvation: no source gives separate figures for either.

### The tin-button myth
- Tin pest would take "maybe 18 months at lowered temperatures to result in appreciable flaking". The Vilnius buttons were "many made of tin alloy and still legible after 190 years in the ground". "Alloying tin with just 5 percent lead is enough to keep the problem at bay." [Straight Dope](https://www.straightdope.com/21343875/where-did-we-get-the-idea-crazy-people-think-they-re-napoleon-plus-did-tin-disease-contribute-to-nap)
- The story comes from Le Couteur and Burreson's book *Napoleon's Buttons* [Univ. Trento](https://mattheory.physics.unitn.it/Materials/Napoleon.html), unquoted.

### What a time-traveller should bring (inference)
- The main killers (typhus, trench fever, relapsing fever, dysentery and paratyphoid) spread through lice and dirty water. That points to delousing (steam or hot-air treatment of clothes, insecticide powder, spare clothing) and water safety (boiling, plus oral rehydration salts) as the top priorities, with food and forage depots after them. Steam delousing is reported to have stopped Serbia's 1915 typhus epidemic [Montana State](https://www.montana.edu/historybug/napoleon/typhus-russia.html), unquoted. Antibiotics would also help but are a lower priority. All of this is unverified as a ranking.
