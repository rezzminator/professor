# RR — Where can a startup legitimately buy the per-property data Walter Living shows Dutch buyers, and does Walter sell it?

Question: Map where a startup can LEGITIMATELY obtain the per-property research data that Walter Living (walterliving.com) shows Dutch home buyers — fair value estimate, WOZ value, sale/price history, energy label, noise, foundation risk, neighbourhood stats — and whether Walter Living itself sells it. Questions: (1) Does Walter Living offer an API, data licence, B2B or partner product? Where does Walter Living itself get its data? (2) For each data type, name the source, access method, price, licence and rate limits — BAG/PDOK; Kadaster transaction prices; WOZ; EP-online; AVM vendors (Altum AI, Matrixian, Calcasa, brainbay); RIVM/Atlas Leefomgeving, KCAF/FunderMaps, Klimaateffectatlas; CBS Wijken en Buurten, Leefbaarometer; and LISTINGS supply given Funda has no public API. (3) Which are free/open and commercially usable, which need a contract? In scope: NL purchase market, 2024-2026 access terms. Not in scope: scraping techniques, rentals, building the app, code repositories.

## Answer

Almost everything in a Walter-style property report is buyable legitimately: BAG is free and public-domain, Kadaster sells sale prices per query at listed tariffs, EP-online publishes bulk energy labels, and one vendor — Altum AI at €100/month — resells AVM + WOZ + transaction + neighbourhood data self-service to a startup with no contract. The two genuinely gated pieces are **live for-sale listings** (brainbay forbids bulk delivery to commercial parties; the only routes are Tiara certification at ~€5.5k setup + ~€16k/year, or per-broker opt-in through Realworks' Wonen API) and **WOZ at scale** (the public loket bars automated querying and Kadaster's WOZ API is municipality-only). Walter Living itself sells nothing B2B — no API, no data licence, no partner product appears on any page checked; it is a consumer subscription (€29/mo, or €2,950 at closing) built on public sources.

## Load-bearing facts

**Walter Living sells nothing B2B, and buys nothing exotic.** Its own FAQ states it collects data "uit uitgebreide openbare bronnen, zoals het Kadaster, CBS, RIVM en Open Overheidsdata" and names no Kadaster licence or product ([FAQ](https://walterliving.com/nl/en/faq/waar-haalt-walter-de-data-vandaan)). Its pricing and about pages show only two consumer plans — Walter Plus €29/mo and Walter All-in-One €2,950 at closing — with no API, data licence, white-label, makelaar tool or affiliate programme. Its one named partnership, with insurer Centraal Beheer, is a co-branded consumer "Woningrapport" pilot, not a data deal ([announcement](https://walterliving.com/nl/en/blog/walter-living-en-centraal-beheer-starten-samenwerking)).

**The free-and-commercially-usable tier is real and substantial.** BAG under CC Public Domain Mark 1.0, CBS wijk/buurt statistics under CC-BY 4.0, Leefbaarometer open download, and EP-online's public bulk energy-label files together cover buildings, neighbourhood, liveability and energy labels at zero marginal cost.

**Three things cost money per query rather than per contract:** Kadaster sale prices (€0,45/address), Kadaster maatwerk ownership lists (from €80 + €0,12/object), and an AVM. Altum AI is the only vendor found publishing a startup-scale price card — €100/month for ~5,000 calls covering Woningwaarde/AVM, WOZ, Woningdata, Buurtdata, Verduurzaming and Transactie APIs ([altum.ai/prijzen](https://altum.ai/prijzen/)). brainbay and Matrixian are contact-sales-only with no published pricing; Calcasa sells reports, not a data feed.

**Listings are the hard wall, and it is a contract wall, not a technical one.** brainbay will not bulk-deliver to commercial parties at all; the two lawful paths are Tiara certification (~€5,500 setup + ~€15,996/year, indexed) or the Realworks Wonen API where each individual broker authorizes your developer-ID against their own listings. The second is the only one a pre-revenue startup can start on today, and it scales by sales effort per makelaar office, not by one signature.

**WOZ at scale is genuinely closed.** The public loket is human-facing and automated mass querying is not permitted; Kadaster's WOZ API requires an OIN and PKIo certificate, is for municipalities, and its data may only be used for tax collection. The practical startup route to WOZ is therefore a reseller — Altum's WOZ API — not the source. *(These WOZ specifics rest on search snippets; the wozwaardeloket.nl disclaimer fetch failed — see below.)*

**Risk data is the weakest-documented layer.** RIVM's Atlas API requires registration but publishes no licence, no rate limit and no commercial-use policy; its documented payload is map metadata (themes, permalinks, UUIDs) rather than per-address values. FunderMaps is a commercial B.V. whose published terms are generic delivery conditions with no API, pricing or redistribution rights disclosed.
