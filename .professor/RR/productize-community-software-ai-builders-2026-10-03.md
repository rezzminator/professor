# RR — Should AI Builders Club productize its community software? (product/platform-from-community lens, wave 2)

Question: Wave 2 of a study on the next best move for AI Builders Club. Lens: PRODUCT / PLATFORM FROM COMMUNITY. Cases: Startup Grind → Bevy, AI Tinkerers, MLH acquiring DEV/Forem, Cerebral Valley, Buildspace (failure contrast); plus the community-tooling market (Luma, Orbit, Common Room, Circle, Commsor, Meetup). Questions: (1) when Startup Grind's tooling became sellable, first buyer, equity/relationship kept, where Bevy is now; (2) what AI Tinkerers' platform does and whether it is licensed or sold; (3) why MLH bought DEV and how it played out; (4) what Cerebral Valley charges for enterprise hackathons, jobs and platform; (5) why Buildspace closed despite revenue, why Orbit shut down, what that says about selling community software; (6) is there a gap for an AI-native WhatsApp-first "community OS" that operators would pay for — market size and pricing evidence.

## Answer

Only one case turned community tooling into a real software business, and it did so by selling to enterprises. Startup Grind's tool became sellable in early 2016, when its first customer was Aging 2.0 ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/the-inside-story-of-bevy-from-startup-grind-to-series-c)). The spin-out, Bevy, now sells chapter-network software to companies such as Google, Salesforce, Etsy and Atlassian ([Yahoo Finance/Bevy release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bevy-acquires-intros-ai-launch-110000380.html)). Every other AI-community peer makes its money from sponsors or services, not software:
- AI Tinkerers: "not monetized", with sponsors paying for venue and food ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/)).
- Cerebral Valley: event production, technical recruiting and compute brokering ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/cerebralvalley)).
- Buildspace: about $1.5M a year in sponsorship revenue by 2022 ([Almanac](https://www.openalmanac.org/w/founders-inc/the-night-buildspace-closed)).

The venture-funded community-software companies were each bought or wound down:
- Orbit was sunset within 90 days of Postman buying it ([Postman](https://blog.postman.com/announcing-postman-has-acquired-orbit/)).
- Commsor raised at a $450M valuation, reached only about $8.4M in revenue, and its products were wound down into The Swarm ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/commsor)).
- Common Room went to Zoom as AI sales tooling ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/zoom-acquires-common-room-ai-sales)).

A WhatsApp-first assistant faces two platform walls. WhatsApp's official Groups API caps a group at 8 participants ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/groups)), and Meta now bars open-domain AI assistants on WhatsApp ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban)). The prices it would compete with are low: $59 a month for Luma Plus ([Luma](https://luma.com/pricing)) and $49 a month for Heartbeat's entry plan ([Heartbeat](https://www.heartbeat.chat/pricing)).

Verdict (inference from the map): building a SaaS company that sells the software to other community operators would be a distraction. Two narrower moves are credible: license the chapter-network stack to companies that sponsor or run developer chapters, as Bevy does, or use the software as the delivery advantage behind sponsor-paid services through AI Builders Ventures.

### 1. Startup Grind → Bevy (partial)

**When it became sellable, and who bought it.**
- Andersen started showing the tool to others in early 2016: "The first person I showed it to became our first customer, Aging 2.0." ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/the-inside-story-of-bevy-from-startup-grind-to-series-c))
- The spin-out came in June 2017: "In June of 2017, with a handful of enterprise customers, we raised $1M in angel funding and split the team in half, keeping some on SG and the other half going to Bevy." ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/how-my-startup-grind-led-to-a-bevy-of-new-possibilities))
- The same blog says the round valued the company at $10M ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/the-inside-story-of-bevy-from-startup-grind-to-series-c)).

**What Startup Grind kept.**
- "Startup Grind became a shareholder of the new entity, having incubated Bevy's technology and team." ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/how-my-startup-grind-led-to-a-bevy-of-new-possibilities))
- Today Bevy is "a sister company and a stakeholder" ([Startup Grind blog](https://blog.startupgrind.com/the-inside-story-of-bevy-from-startup-grind-to-series-c)).
- No percentage stake was found in four rounds of digging.
- Andersen leads both: he is "co-founder/CEO of Bevy", and Startup Grind is "a community of 600 Chapters in 125 countries" ([Startup Grind blog](https://www.startupgrind.com/blog/its-a-startup-grind-and-we-all-know-it/)).

**Funding.**
- Series A: "$6.4 million by the summer of 2018" ([Foundersuite](https://blog.foundersuite.com/why-the-founder-of-startup-grind-decided-to-turn-to-the-dark-side-and-raise-vc-for-his-new-startup-bevy/)).
- Series B: "$15 million in Series B funding", "led by Accel", announced 19 May 2020 ([TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/19/bevy-series-b/)). TechCrunch's own 2021 article calls this the "May 2019" Series B, which looks like a typo.
- Series C: "a $40 million Series C" on 24 March 2021, with "the startup is valued at $325 million" and "has raised $60 million" in total ([TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/24/bevy-raises-40m-series-c-with-20-coming-from-black-investors/)).

**Acquisitions.**
- CMX, the community for community professionals, on 11 February 2019. "The financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed." David Spinks became Bevy's VP of community ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/02/11/bevy-acquires-cmx/)).
- Eventtus, July 2021 ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/bevy/__1PMKIpg6NSIQKcCCITAXZUUmfXr_vy4nLaJSy_vnEe0), unquoted).
- Intros AI, 8 July 2025, described as "Bevy's third strategic acquisition"; customers named are Google, Salesforce, Etsy and Atlassian ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bevy-acquires-intros-ai-launch-110000380.html)).

**Bevy today (estimates).**
- Getlatka: "In 2024, Bevy's revenue reached $15.9M." "previously reported $10.8M in 2023" ([Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/bevy)).
- Headcount: "approximately 65 people as of 2026, down from 92 in 2023". The page also lists "Reached 219 employees (December 2021)". No layoff announcement was found.
- Customers: "Bevy serves 30K customers." This is an estimate site.
- Bevy's own claim: "more than 30,000 event organizers" ([Bevy blog](https://bevy.com/b/blog/the-inside-story-of-bevy), unquoted at verify).
- Tracxn still lists Bevy at Series C stage as of September 2026, so no sale of Bevy has been reported (unquoted).

**Pricing.**
- OMR lists Starter "$49/month", Pro "$1,000/month" and Enterprise "$1,500/month" ([OMR](https://omr.com/en/reviews/product/bevy/pricing)).
- Enterprise chapter networks are "charged according to the total number of groups on the platform" ([Skift](https://meetings.skift.com/reviews/bevys-community-event-management-platform-review/)).
- The buyer is the company running the chapter network, such as Google or Salesforce, not the volunteer chapters.

### 2. AI Tinkerers (settled)

**What the platform does.**
- "Heitzeberg built the platform that powers AI Tinkerers." ([GeekWire, 2024-04-19](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/))
- It screens applicants: "The waitlist for many AI Tinkerers meetups is often larger than the number of people given the OK to attend."
- It runs demo submissions: "Of more than 650 AI tech demos submitted for consideration, 257 have have been presented."
- The organizer page lists event pages, RSVP, reminders, check-in, screening, speaker workflows and media ([AI Tinkerers](https://aitinkerers.org/all_cities), unquoted).

**What it runs on.**
- The site footer reads "Created with DREAM.page" ([AI Tinkerers jobs](https://aitinkerers.org/jobs)).
- Heitzeberg launched DREAM.page himself: "Today I launched DREAM.page, an AI-first personal publishing platform" ([Currently Obsessed](https://currentlyobsessed.com/p/launching-dream-page)). DREAM.page now markets itself as "The Community and Events Management Platform for Curated Communities" ([dream.page](https://dream.page)).
- No pricing and no outside customers were found. There is no evidence the platform is licensed or sold to others.

**Sponsors buy access through the platform.**
- The VIP dinners page offers a "Sponsor Platform" with "Vetting Control: Hand-pick ideal attendees with ICP matching, AI scoring" ([VIP dinners](https://aitinkerers.org/vip-dinners)).

**Scale.**
- "264+ cities worldwide" ([sponsor page](https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor), undated).
- "Nearly 8,000 members have taken part" ([GeekWire, 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/)).

**How it makes money.**
- "AI Tinkerers is not monetized. The only money … comes from sponsors who pick up the tab for a meetup venue and the food and drink." ([GeekWire, 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/))

- The January 2026 capture showed "Price: Contact us" and a budget form ranging from "$1–3k" to "$250k+" ([Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20260128020331/https://aitinkerers.org/sponsor)).
- Other lines: a "Sponsored Jobs" board ([jobs](https://aitinkerers.org/jobs)), a talent/recruiting service, and the AIT Fund, which makes "First checks into technical founders" ([aitfund.ai](https://aitfund.ai)).

### 3. MLH acquires DEV/Forem (settled)

**The deal.**
- MLH announced the acquisition of DEV on 18 February 2026, describing itself as "reaching 1M+ developers" and DEV as "the 3M-strong digital community" ([MLH](https://blog.mlh.com/major-league-hacking-mlh-acquires-developer-community-platform-dev-02-18-2026)). No terms were disclosed.

**Why.**
- Mike Swift: "MLH is where you build and break things in person. DEV is where those lessons live on." and "In the AI era, everyone is a beginner again" ([MLH](https://blog.mlh.com/major-league-hacking-mlh-acquires-developer-community-platform-dev-02-18-2026)).
- Ben Halpern: "A continuous path from conversation to creation. Online and IRL aren't separate worlds." ([DEV](https://dev.to/devteam/a-new-chapter-dev-is-joining-forces-with-major-league-hacking-mlh-3kfd))

**What happened to Forem.**
- "MLH and DEV will serve as stewards for Forem, the open-source project powering DEV." ([MLH](https://blog.mlh.com/major-league-hacking-mlh-acquires-developer-community-platform-dev-02-18-2026))
- Halpern: the partnership is "a clarification of the distinction between DEV and Forem", fostering Forem "as a dedicated open source project". He also said "The original team isn't going anywhere" ([DEV](https://dev.to/devteam/a-new-chapter-dev-is-joining-forces-with-major-league-hacking-mlh-3kfd)).

**DEV/Forem had tried to sell its software.**
- DEV raised an "$11.5M Series A led by Mayfield", after about $950k from angels ([DEV, 2019](https://dev.to/devteam/special-announcement-from-the-dev-founders-hel)).
- In 2021 it launched Forem Cloud, a premium hosted service for "larger companies that can invest at premium pricing" ([DEV, 2021](https://dev.to/devteam/forem-self-host-is-now-officially-supported-16h0), unquoted).
- The outcome: the community site went to an events organization, and the software was split off as open source. Forem Cloud's revenue was not found.

**MLH's model.**
- "more than 2,000 annual events … more than 100,000 participants a year"; "We are revenue partners with companies interested in reaching this important audience"; "all the events are free" ([Forbes, 2020-01-19](https://www.forbes.com/sites/dereknewton/2020/01/19/hacking-grows-up/)).

**Verdict (lead's inference):** Productizing as a standalone SaaS sold to other community operators is a distraction for AI Builders Club now, for four reasons:
- Its only proven buyer is the enterprise running chapters, which Bevy already owns.
- Every venture-funded community-software peer since 2022 was acquired, wound down or pivoted to go-to-market software.
- Its AI-community peers chose sponsor and service revenue and kept their software in-house.
- The WhatsApp layer runs into Meta's 8-member group cap and its ban on open-domain bots.

The credible version is narrow. Treat the software as the delivery engine for sponsor-paid programs under AI Builders Ventures. Test a per-chapter license with one or two companies that already sponsor its events before building anything for the open market.
