# RR — Who is "AI Builders", which analogous communities scaled or became something bigger, and which should wave 2 study

Question: Mission (head research of a two-wave study): find the next best strategic move for the "AI Builders" community. You are wave 1. Your output picks the cases that wave 2 (parallel deep-dives) will study, so cover breadth and rank carefully. Part A — profile us (identity, site/handles, founding, cities, member counts, cadence, sponsors, model, rivals ruled out, confidence). Part B — analogues: closest comparable AI-native communities and classic tech communities that turned into something bigger (company, VC fund, accelerator, conference business, school, media, platform), including failures, each with size, origin, outcome and a 1–5 closeness score. Part C — wave-2 plan: group the strongest ~12–16 cases into 4 clusters by lens (revenue model; turning into an entity; growth & expansion; product/platform from community), with 3–5 questions each. Return the saved RR path, our profile with identity confidence, the ranked analogue table, the 4-cluster plan, and open rabbit holes. Cite every number with source and date.

## Answer

"AI Builders" is the Amsterdam-headquartered AI Builders Club, a nonprofit founded in 2023 ([LinkedIn](https://nl.linkedin.com/company/aibuilders)). It reports "9 cities running", "114 nights already run" and "10,400 people in the community" ([aibuilders.club/contribute](https://aibuilders.club/contribute)). It is funded through an Open Collective hosted by Open Collective Europe Foundation, which shows €11,603.00 collected ([Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/aibuilders)). Its manifesto already plans a "Software + Community" club, with a separate "AI Builders Ventures" for paid programs that "funds the Club" ([manifesto](https://aibuilders.club/manifesto)).

The closest analogues are two:
- **AI Tinkerers.** It is "not monetized", sponsors cover each meetup, and its founder built the platform that powers it ([GeekWire, 2024-04-19](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattle-tech-vet-calls-rapidly-growing-ai-tinkerers-meetups-the-new-homebrew-computer-club-for-ai/), UNCHECKED). It now lists "136,137 Members" across "264 Global Cities" ([aitinkerers.org](https://aitinkerers.org/)).

### Part C — Wave-2 plan (16 cases, 4 clusters)

**Cluster 1 — Revenue model.** Cases: AI Tinkerers, MLOps Community, PyData/NumFOCUS, Pavilion, Women Who Code (failure).
1. What do AI Tinkerers' Global Partnership and Local Chapter sponsorships actually cost, and how is the money split between HQ and chapters? Is the unverified $5k/event and $25k/quarter claim real?
2. What share of MLOps Community revenue comes from sponsorship, workshops, jobs and events, and roughly how much does it make a year?
3. How much does a PyData/NumFOCUS conference net per city at the £4k–£17k tiers, and what fiscal-host fee does NumFOCUS take?
4. Pavilion: paid membership plus chapter revenue share. What did cutting the share do to chapter leads and retention?
5. Women Who Code: which funding sources dried up in 2023–24, and what fixed-cost ratio broke it?

**Cluster 2 — Turning into an entity.** Cases: Slush (Slush Oy), AI Engineer (Software 3.0 Inc), South Park Commons, Encode Club, On Deck (stall).
1. How did Slush move from Aaltoes volunteers to Slush Oy? Who owns it (a foundation or the society), and how are volunteers treated?
2. What entity and ownership sit behind AI Engineer, and how do its licensed partner events (Paris, Singapore and others) split revenue?
3. SPC: when and how did the community add a fund? What do members get, and does the fund finance the community?
4. Encode Club: which programs justified VC (a $5M seed), and what does it sell to sponsors and protocols?
5. On Deck: which paid product lines failed first, and how did the Tiger-backed overextension unwind?

**Cluster 3 — Growth and expansion (chapters, franchising, flywheels).** Cases: City.AI, Founder Institute, HackerNest, Codebar.
1. How did City.AI recruit and govern 70 city ambassadors, and what happened to it after 2019? Is it still active, closed, or Dutch-registered?
2. Founder Institute: how do the 2.5% Equity Collective and the 1% Local Leader share work in practice, and could that model carry AI Builders' virtual shares?
3. HackerNest: why did a 34-city volunteer franchise stall (if it did)? What was the "splinter cell" playbook?
4. Codebar: chapter launch criteria, the partner-tier economics (726 partners) and how volunteers are retained.
5. Across all four: chapter approval rate (Startup Grind's "About 10%"), brand control, and how they handle organizer churn.

**Cluster 4 — Product or platform from community.** Cases: Startup Grind → Bevy, AI Tinkerers' platform, MLH → DEV, Cerebral Valley (CV platform and enterprise hackathons). Buildspace is the failure contrast.
