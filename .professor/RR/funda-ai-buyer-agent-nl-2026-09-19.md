# RR — Does any existing app run the full AI interview → Funda scan → research → notify → book-the-viewing flow for a Dutch home buyer?

Question: find whether an existing app/service delivers this end-to-end flow for a home BUYER in the Netherlands — (1) an AI chatbot that interviews the buyer and extracts their real preferences into deep filters beyond Funda's own; (2) continuous scanning of Funda for new listings; (3) per-candidate research (neighbourhood, price history, WOZ, energy label, bidding context) with the AI filters applied; (4) email/notification "something good is out there — should I book a viewing?"; (5) on yes, booking the viewing with the makelaar on a date from the buyer's calendar. In scope: Dutch-market products (Funda's own alerts/AI, Walter Living, Huispedia, Huislijn, Pararius, Jaap, aankoopmakelaar-tech startups, auto-viewing-request bots, open-source Funda scrapers/notifiers, Telegram/Discord bots) plus international analogues (Zillow/Redfin/Rightmove AI agents). Per product: which of stages 1–5, pricing, status, URL. Also: Funda ToS and anti-scraping posture (official API or partner feed?), how viewing requests actually work on Funda, and legal/GDPR notes on automating requests on a user's behalf. Not in scope: rental-only tools except as a demonstration of the auto-respond pattern; building the app; this repository.

---

## Answer

**No product in the Dutch market runs the whole flow.** The market splits cleanly in two: buyer platforms (Roofmatch, Walter Living) do stages 2–4 well and explicitly stop before stage 5, while the only Dutch tools that actually *send* a request on your behalf are rental concierges (Rentbird Plus, and possibly Uprent) with no AI preference-interview and no buyer-side research. Stage 1 — an AI that interviews a buyer to extract preferences they cannot articulate — is essentially unbuilt in NL; Roofmatch does that step with a human on a 30-minute video call.

The surprise finding is that **stage 5 is not the blocker it looks like**: Realworks' Move.nl *Bezichtigingsplanner* already gives real self-service viewing-slot booking for Funda-listed properties in the makelaar's own CRM. The blocker is stage 2 — Funda's terms forbid scraping outright and there is no official public API.

## Load-bearing facts

- **Roofmatch refuses stage 5 on principle**, not for lack of tech: the buyer does their own viewings because the selling agent is already there. Its stage-1 intake is a human video call on locatie/budget/type/tijdlijn — not a preference-extracting AI ([Roofmatch](https://www.roofmatch.com/en/aankoopmakelaar/utrecht)). Roof Radar's claim is supply *speed* — listings 24–36h before Funda/Pararius ([roofradar.app](https://www.roofradar.app/)).
- **Walter Living is the deepest stage-3 engine** — fair value, comps, WOZ, energy labels, noise, foundation risk via "Walter Maps", plus price-drop watching on every Funda listing. Its paid buying agent is human support *after* a viewing exists ([walterliving.com](https://walterliving.com/nl/en/)).
- **Stage 5 infrastructure already exists in NL.** Realworks' Move.nl *Bezichtigingsplanner* lets a buyer pick an open slot inside blocks the makelaar configures, syncing straight into the Realworks CRM; per Realworks, "the online planning flow is already widely used for properties on Funda, where requests are picked up and scheduled directly without manual follow-up." Free for offices on Realworks Connect/Pro, also embeddable on the agent's own site ([realworks.nl](https://www.realworks.nl/bezichtigingsplanner), [Connect announcement](https://www.realworks.nl/post/bezichtigingsplanner-standaard-in-connect), [FAQ](https://faq.realworks.nl/article/hoe-werkt-de-bezichtigingsplanner)). Kolibri, Stepping Stones and Brixxs were not checked.
- **Funda's terms ban the core mechanic by name.** The gebruiksvoorwaarden (amended 6 March 2026) read: *"Het is je niet toegestaan om zonder voorafgaande schriftelijke toestemming van Funda, het platform of het daarop beschikbaar gestelde materiaal te verveelvoudigen, te kopiëren, te wijzigen, te distribueren, te verspreiden, te 'reverse engineeren', te 'scrapen', te decompileren, te framen, 'te dataminen', beschikbaar te stellen, te gebruiken voor het trainen van (generatieve) kunstmatige intelligentie of op andere wijze te gebruiken en/of te exploiteren."* Scraping, data mining and AI training, all three, without prior written consent (archived copy of [funda.nl/voorwaarden-en-beleid/gebruiksvoorwaarden](https://www.funda.nl/voorwaarden-en-beleid/gebruiksvoorwaarden/)).
- **robots.txt is not the wall; the contract and the CAPTCHA are.** funda.nl/robots.txt `Allow`s `/zoeken/`, `/detail/`, `/informatie/`, `/makelaar-zoeken/`, `/voormakelaars/` for `User-agent: *` and only disallows `/account/` and combined multi-filter search URLs (`/zoeken/koop/*,*`, `/zoeken/huur/*,*`), publishing a sitemap. Enforcement is contractual plus live bot detection — six separate fetch attempts across two diggers hit Funda's verification interstitial, including through web.archive.org paths.
- **No official public API.** Third-party scrapers (Apify, ScrapingBee, Browserless, ScrapingBot) exist commercially, which is itself evidence there is no legitimate feed. [pyfunda](https://github.com/0xMH/pyfunda) hits Funda's undocumented `*.funda.io` mobile-app JSON endpoints instead of HTML, and reports that funda-scraper's listing-date field has been blocked since Q4 2023. Whether fundainbusiness.nl offers a partner data feed to professionals is **unverified**.
- **The adverse precedent is Innoweb, not NVM.** In *Innoweb v Wegener* (CJEU C-202/12, 19 Dec 2013) a dedicated meta-search engine ("GasPedaal") running ~100,000 automated queries a day against AutoTrack and republishing results was held to *re-utilise* a substantial part of the database under Art. 7 of Directive 96/9/EC, distinguished from a general search engine's incidental indexing ([SCL summary](https://www.scl.org/2984-database-right-innoweb-v-wegener-cjeu-judgment/), [EUR-Lex C-202/12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62012CJ0202)). The Dutch *NVM v Zoekallehuizen.nl* case went the other way — Hof Arnhem 2006 (ECLI:NL:GHARN:2006:AY0089) held individual makelaars could not claim protection over listings they had themselves published, and found market-power abuse by NVM ([RTV Utrecht](https://www.rtvutrecht.nl/nieuws/108473/nvm-verliest-zaak-tegen-zoekallehuizennl), [Wieringa Advocaten](https://www.wieringa-advocaten.nl/nl/weblog/2006/07/04/zoekallehuizennl-mag-ook-van-hof/), [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/gerechtshof-nvm-misbruikt-macht)) — but that case scraped *scattered individual agent sites* with no single database investor. Funda is a single aggregator that invested in compiling the set, which makes it Wegener, not the 2006 defendants. **Note: the legal digger's primary-source fetches all failed (EUR-Lex ×2, rechtspraak.nl, SCL all returned empty or 403); the case-law detail above rests on search snippets, and no verbatim holding was retrieved.**
- **The US pattern works because the brokerage owns the agent.** Redfin's "Book It Now" is instant because the tour is with a *Redfin* agent on Redfin's own payroll and MLS/lockbox rails ([redfin.com/news](https://www.redfin.com/news/redfin-book-it-now/)); Zillow's AI mode books a tour inside the AI experience but the press release never says whether that is instant-confirm or a request a human fulfils. Neither structure exists in NL, where the listing makelaar is a third party you must ask.

## The closest match, named plainly

**Roofmatch is the closest single product** — it is the only one covering stages 2, 3 and 4 with genuine automation, and its Roof Radar arm attacks the hardest part of the problem (supply, earlier than Funda). But it deliberately hands stage 5 back to the buyer and does stage 1 with a human. **Walter Living is the closest on data depth** (stage 3) and likewise stops at stage 5. **Rentbird Plus is the only Dutch service that truly performs stage 5's "act on my behalf"** — at €1,495 + €299, with a human team as spokesperson, in the rental market, with no stage 1 or 3.

## The gap nobody fills

The whole right-hand side of the flow, joined up: **a preference-extracting AI interview (1) wired to an automated, calendar-aware viewing booking (5)**. Every Dutch player treats the viewing as the moment a human takes over, and every AI player treats preference capture as a search box rather than an interview. Globally only Zillow's AI mode claims 1 and 5 together, and it does so on a portal that owns its own agent network. In NL the two ends exist separately — Realworks' Bezichtigingsplanner is a bookable calendar sitting behind Funda listings right now, and nobody on the buyer side is booking into it programmatically.

The second, harder gap: **legitimate stage-2 supply**. Funda bans scraping by name, offers no public API, and an AI-training clause was added by 6 March 2026. A dedicated Funda meta-searcher is close to the Innoweb fact pattern, and Funda's explicit ToS reservation likely defeats a DSM Art. 4 TDM defence. The viable routes are partnership with Funda, sourcing from makelaar CRMs/feeds directly (as Roof Radar's pre-Funda supply implies), or user-authorised access to the user's own Funda account — none of them a scraper.

## Open questions

- **Funda's own buyer features are unverified.** Two diggers were blocked by Funda's bot wall; saved-search alert frequency, any AI/natural-language search, any chatbot, and any paid buyer tier all remain unconfirmed. This needs a human browser session, not a fetch tool.
- **How a Funda viewing request actually leaves the page** — contact form vs. embedded Bezichtigingsplanner widget vs. "Plan een bezichtiging" — was never verified from a Funda page. Only the Realworks side is confirmed.
- **Huispedia, Huislijn, Pararius, Jaap and Move.nl's consumer side were not looked up at all.** Budget went to Roofmatch, Walter Living and Homeup. Absence here is an unsearched category, not a finding.
- **Uprent's autonomous auto-apply claim is unverified** — uprent.nl returned 403; the claim that it auto-fills and submits applications including on Funda comes from a single secondary source.
- **Zillow AI mode's stage 5 is ambiguous**: instant-confirm or routed to a human agent? The press release does not say, and its stages 2–3 are unconfirmed.
- **All primary legal sources failed to fetch.** No verbatim Innoweb holding, no EUR-Lex text of DSM Art. 3/4 or the AI Act Art. 50, no Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens guidance. The GDPR analysis (service operator as controller, makelaar as independent controller on receipt, Art. 50 disclosure that a message is AI-sent) and the volmacht/Wft points are reasoned, unverified, and should be treated as questions for a Dutch lawyer rather than findings.
- **Whether Funda has ever enforced the scraping/AI clause** — no litigation or enforcement action was found, but this was not searched directly.
- Kolibri, Stepping Stones and Brixxs viewing-booking capability: not checked. If they match Realworks, stage 5 coverage across the NL market is much wider than one vendor.
